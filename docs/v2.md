The chief designer's decision document, 2026-10-12.

**Inputs:**
- four audits of the WebGPU port: `audit-render.md`, `audit-liquids.md`, `audit-physics.md`, `audit-perf.md`;
- three pitches: `pitch-crisp.md`, `pitch-fidelity.md`, `pitch-lean.md`;
- two judges: `judge-0.md` (player) and `judge-1.md` (GPU engineering);
- `docs/webgpu.md` sections A–F and its Sync log, on `claude/webgpu-port` (976f008);
- the evidence each of these produced (Appendix A).

**How to read the numbers:**
- *est.* is arithmetic from bytes and passes.
- *prototype* comes from `lean/lean-sim.mjs`, rerun by judge 0 with identical results.
- *measured* comes from the audits' runs or from the repo's own records of the user's M5.
- Every *est.* is replaced by a measurement on the user's Mac and iPhone in week 0 and P1.

> **Superseded on the look (2026-10-14, the user's decision).** The game keeps its smooth, realistic look; the
> "crisp blocks" renderer below was rejected as the main look. Blocks survive only as the Plain view (hotkey 0,

### Implemented milestone: compact presentation (opt-in)

`?physics=v2` still selects the incomplete **box-only** prototype; v1 remains the default.
The smooth renderer is retained. Read-only volume, Plain mesh, fields, occupancy, shadow,
GI and picker passes decode authoritative compact cells through one shared projection.
`Header.format` selects the representation; `Header.src` remains a legacy copy index.
Legacy A/B are a compatibility cache exported only at legacy edit/readback boundaries
and mode transitions. Rule writers report changed visible projections without exporting cells.
Awake-but-unchanged halos do not reset render ages. Independent compatibility-dirty bits
accumulate until export, including after sleep.
Paused presentation imports pending edits before consuming compact cells.

**Persistence is not lossless:** the existing save/network format projects fractional liquid
fill to whole cells and drops flux/fall state. Legacy edits still operate on that whole-cell
projection (including invisible fractional liquid). Exact v2 undo snapshots separately retain
all compact words. History entries are format-tagged: restoring a v2 entry while in v2 restores
those words exactly, even after toggling modes; restoring it in v1 uses its lossy projection.
A v1 history entry restored in v2 is imported as legacy whole cells. Switching v2→v1→v2 itself
also projects the running state. Shifted compact undo is explicitly rejected: v2 is box-only.

This integration does **not** complete physics parity or establish any full-frame speedup.
Pressure, heat/reactions/burning, particles and other planned physics remain unimplemented.
Moving, settled and continuous-edit full-frame measurements at equal simulated time, scene,
quality and resolution are still required. Test pages may set `physics.v2.forceCompatibility`
for a same-physics bridged baseline; this is not a user setting.
The first M5 comparison found no meaningful full-frame gain from removing the bridge.
See [completion gates and measurements](v2/completion.md) before claiming performance or feature completion.

Checks: `node tools/v2/compact-check.mjs` (CPU command protocol), then, only with GPU permission,
`node tools/v2/compact-browser-check.mjs --port 5197` (real shader/read/render integration).

## 0. Summary

**This is a rebuild, not a port.** The user's verdict is that today's game is a draft, not a tuned design, and that
porting it faithfully recreates its bugs and slop. The audits agree: the look, the water, the physics and the frame
cost all come from models that were patched rather than fixed.

v2 keeps only the engine room that works:
- the WebGPU foundation and the app shell;
- brick-granular sleeping, box-scoped edits and the determinism rules;
- the world generator, and the elements and scenes as content.

Everything the player sees and feels is designed from scratch.

**Direction: crisp blocks on a lean engine.** Both judges reached this design from opposite ends:
- judge 0: the CRISP pitch as the base, plus grafts from LEAN and FIDELITY;
- judge 1: LEAN's engine drawn with CRISP's frame, plus grafts from FIDELITY.

### What it will look and feel like
- **Every cell is a block you can read.**
  - Each cell takes one of its element's colour tones (TPT's per-particle jitter).
  - Up close, each face is 16×16 pixel art at Minecraft's density.
  - Sun shadows are sharp and every crease gets soft corner shading.
  - Light works like Minecraft's sky and block light: lava lights a cave orange.
  - One sky is both the backdrop and the light source.
  - There is no clay look and no shimmer. Without TAA, each frame is final the moment it's drawn.
- **Water acts like water.**
  - It finds its level through pipes and U-bends, and a hole throws one arcing jet.
  - Spills stop as puddles with an edge.
  - Mud, slime and lava hold slopes.
  - Splashes are energy-bounded and every liquid can foam.
  - Anything under water is tinted by its real depth.
- **Things move honestly, on one clock.**
  - A slab falls as a slab and a throw lands where it was aimed.
  - Heaps form at each material's own angle, then sleep.
  - Fire spreads by contact.
  - Blasts are round and shielded by walls, and their shock waves are visible.
- **It stays The Powder Toy.**
  - Every element is still paintable as cells, gases and FIRE included.
  - The heat, pressure, flow and X-ray views stay.
  - Two things are new: a cutaway view, and a "crunch" style toggle (Cruelty Squad).

### The 5 biggest changes
1. **Raster the cells' faces instead of raymarching blurred clay.** Edges are smoothed by 4× MSAA kept in tile memory
   instead of TAA.
2. **One lighting model for everything:**
   - a sun shadow map;
   - per-cell sky and block light;
   - corner AO baked by the mesher;
   - one sky;
   - one emission function.
3. **Liquids store a fill level and flow through each face, with pressure.** The prototype levels a U-tube within
   2 cm and rests every spill. **Anything flying is a particle** (Noita).
4. **One 60 Hz clock with three kinds of motion:**
   - column fall for vertical runs;
   - contact rules in 2×2×2 blocks;
   - particles for sideways flight.
5. **Rest is reported by the rules themselves, and all work keys on visible change.** A settled world with a still
   camera costs nothing.

### Expected performance (est.)

| | today | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| MacBook Pro M5, busy lab | 18–28 ms a frame (36–55 fps) | ~2 ms (120 Hz with the GPU idle ~75 % of the time) |
| iPhone 17 Pro Max | ~20–35 fps (est.), cut-down look | ~3–4 ms (locked 60 Hz, same look, ~1–1.5 W) |
| GPU memory at 128³ | 408 MB at boot, 600+ MB after 3 strokes | ~150–230 MB Mac, ~120–170 MB iPhone |
| settled world, still camera | nothing (kept) | nothing |
| shader code per session | ~1.7 MB; a 5,400-line view | ≤ 400 KB; no kernel over 700 lines |

The plan is in section 11. The user's decisions are in section 13.

## 1. The decision

### 1.1 What is actually wrong today: root causes, not symptoms

**Look** (`audit-render.md`):
- The world is drawn as clay. 30 cm cells are blurred into rounded isosurfaces, then painted with millimetre-scale
  procedural texture. From the default 39–103 m god view almost all of that texture has already faded to a flat
  average.
- Seventeen elements have hand-written hero shaders while ~73 are flat colour.
- Ten lighting models disagree with each other:
  - a navy backdrop sits behind a scene lit by a blue sky;
  - hot matter glows 3–6× brighter in reflections than in direct view;
  - water tints what's behind it grey instead of in colour.
- Several noisy estimators only converge under 109 frames of TAA, so lava light flickers ±20 %.

**Liquids** (`audit-liquids.md`):
- Liquids are a powder automaton with velocity "kicks". There is no pressure and no fill level.
  - A U-tube rests with its arms 20 cells apart and certifies itself idle.
  - Pipes clog.
  - Puddles and partial layers never rest.
  - A landing multiplies kinetic energy by 160–210.
- The repo history shows six attempts in three days to make one pour look like water.

**Physics** (`audit-physics.md`):
- Each cell carries a velocity that a 2×2×2 coin-flip automaton can't actually move, so throws travel 33–57 % of
  their distance and a slab smears over 21 cells.
- "Pressure" is a max-plus influence field. Blasts come out octahedral, pressure grows 680× from nothing, and there
  is neither vacuum nor containment.
- Density is used as a mass, so gold nuggets punch through steel.
- Fire is purely a temperature threshold, so two 1,000 °C flames don't light wood.
- The rest test is a hand-written mirror of the move rules, and it's wrong. Heaps and CO₂ pools never sleep.
- The grid, the heat and the first-person player run on three different clocks.

**Performance** (`audit-perf.md`):
- The sim runs 4 steps per frame at ~167 B per awake cell per step.
- Every derived product is redone every frame anything moves:
  - the shadow map (262K rays);
  - GI;
  - the field pipeline, which reprocesses 125 bricks for 13 frames per change.
- The view re-marches every pixel and keeps doing so for 109 frames after any change, a pointer move included.
- 408–650 MB resident; 1.7 MB of WGSL compiled per session.

**The common root.** v1 hid the cell instead of embracing it, and patched symptoms instead of replacing the models.
v2 does the opposite. It shows the cell, and it picks models whose correct behaviour is the default:
- pressure from compression;
- rest from integer fixed points;
- motion from ballistics;
- light from one deterministic function.

### 1.2 The direction: crisp blocks on a lean engine

| from | taken |
|---|---|
| **CRISP** (the frame and the look) | raster face meshes, 4× MSAA resolved in tile memory with no TAA, so a frame is final when drawn; one material function, one light function, one emission function, one sky; gases and FIRE kept as cells; TPT's data views; phase-by-phase shipping with the look first; the max-plus light update and its convergence proof |
| **LEAN** (the engine) | 16 B single-copy state updated in place; column fall + block pass + particles for sideways flight; fill + flux liquids, validated by its CPU prototype; per-cell light grid; 4 B faces whose vertex shader reads the cell live, so temperature, fill and glow changes never remesh; budgets as law with the iPhone as the reference device, a runtime governor and CI caps; TF2's 2 g; crunch mode; cutaway view; reactions over 3 in-block partners; integer heat with a dead band |

### 1.4 What v2 is not doing
- **No per-pixel tracing and no radiance cache** (fidelity). Reasons:
  - it is the worst perf-per-watt choice on TBDR;
  - the cache lags for ~10+ frames in exactly the moments that matter (fires, blasts);
  - moving sand goes aliased under TAA history rejection;
  - its megakernel compile risks a first-load stall in Safari.
- **No TAA or TAAU.** In a world that moves every frame, "final when drawn" beats "converges in 8 frames".
- **No gas species replacing gas cells** (lean).
- **No Mac-only look.** Extras ship only if they fit the iPhone budget, or as an explicit "Ultra" option if the user
  wants one (section 13).
- **No required optional WebGPU features** (subgroups, shader-f16 and so on). Optional fast paths are feature-checked.
- **No porting of v1's rules, constants or look for their own sake.** The 91 elements and 14 reactions are content,
  migrated by script and re-tuned on the bench and the shot sheets.

## 2. House rules

Every later decision follows these.

1. **The cell is the unit of meaning.** Nothing is drawn below a cell except texels of the cell's own pattern.
   Liquids and gases are the only smooth things, because they have a level or a density. The cell size stays 0.30 m:
   the player is 5.5 cells tall, and a 128³ box is a 38 m site.
2. **Data, not branches.** An element is a row. Anything a row can't express becomes a general rule for every row.
   Adding an element is data only, with no exceptions.
3. **One of everything:**
   - one clock and one g;
   - one material function, one light function, one emission function and one sky;
   - one motion law per regime;
   - one implementation, with no CPU twin.
4. **Real units in, declared game multipliers out.**
   - Inputs are in kg, J, m/s, Pa·s and °C.
   - Where play needs unreality, it is one named constant with its factor beside it: 2 g, `HEAT_SPEED` ≈ 10⁴,
     `BLAST_SCALE` = 10⁻³.
   - Numbers are borrowed from a touchstone and cited in a comment (CLAUDE.md).
5. **No estimator that needs more than one frame to be right.** If a term is noisy, it isn't in the game.
6. **Work scales with visible change.** One falling grain costs the bricks it touches, never the world. CI asserts
   the workgroups dispatched per changed brick.
7. **Rest is the rule's own answer.** Every rule is written `if (possible) { busy = true; if (draw < chance)
   apply(); }`. Integer state and dead bands make "nothing possible" reachable.
8. **Incremental is exact or it isn't done.** The mesher, the light grid and sleep each have a full-recompute twin and
   a bit-exact test (kept from the port).
9. **The iPhone is the reference device.** A default that doesn't fit the phone budget doesn't ship anywhere. The Mac
   gets more pixels and 120 Hz, not a different game.
10. **Outcomes, not mechanisms, are tested:**
    - a U-tube levels;
    - a heap sleeps;
    - a throw lands;
    - a flame lights wood.

    Tests that pin a hack's constants are deleted.

## 3. What you see: the renderer

### 3.1 The frame

```
per tick (60 Hz × Speed; only while something is awake)
  compute  particles → column fall → block pass → liquid × 4 substeps → air grid (P4) → activity, masks
  compute  upkeep, only for bricks whose look changed:
           remesh chunks · light grid · gas texture · liquid-top column map
per presented frame (only when the camera, the world or an overlay changed)
  render   shadow map, depth only    (if an opaque face in the sun's view changed or the sun moved; ≤ 30 Hz)
  compute  cull                      (if the camera moved or a chunk was remeshed) → one compacted list per pipeline
  render   OPAQUE pass, 4× MSAA, resolved in tile memory
             sky → faces (opaque, cutout foliage) → flying cubes and droplets → three.js opaque meshes (composite)
             outputs: resolved colour (rgba16f) + linear depth (r32f); the samples are never written to memory
  compute  gas march, ½ res (¼ on iPhone), only where gas bricks are on screen
  render   TRANSLUCENT pass, 1×, reading the resolved colour and depth
             liquid and glass faces → gas composite (depth-aware upsample) → flame sprites → sparks
  compute  bloom (≤ ½ res), histogram, eye adaptation (post's compute pass, kept)
  render   canvas: exposure, AgX, bloom, then overlays (cursor, brush, box edges, legends)
```

This is at most 4 render passes and 3 compute passes per presented frame; today it is 5 render passes and ~76
dispatches. Box mode is one command buffer, and fewer passes are the precondition for retrying 2 frames in flight on
Safari.

| event | work it causes |
|---|---|
| nothing changed | nothing: 0 submits, 0 `requestAnimationFrame` (deep idle, kept) |
| the pointer moved | the canvas pass only: overlays are drawn after tone mapping, depth-tested by hand against the resolved depth |
| the camera moved over a sleeping world | cull + opaque + translucent + post |
| one grain fell | a tick over its bricks + its chunk remeshed + light around it + one frame |
| the sun moved > 0.25° | the shadow map + one frame |
| between ticks at 120 Hz | a redraw only if the camera moved or particles are in flight (interpolated) |

### 3.2 Geometry: faces from a GPU mesher

- **A face is a `u32`:**

  | field | bits | notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | chunk-local xyz | 12 | within a 16³ chunk |
  | direction | 3 | |
  | corner AO | 8 | 4 corners × 2 bits, from Lysenko's 3-neighbour rule (0fps.net, 2013) |
  | convex-edge mask | 4 | |
  | flags | 5 | liquid top, glass, cutout, LOD, spare |

- **What gets a face:**
  - opaque against non-opaque;
  - liquid against air, gas or glass;
  - glass against non-glass;
  - cutout foliage on both sides.
- **No allocator.** Each 16³ chunk owns a fixed slab sized for its worst case: 12,288 faces, 48 KB, 25 MB at 128³.
  One workgroup per chunk counts faces per direction, prefix-sums the counts and writes them.
- **When a chunk is remeshed.** Only when an id or occupancy inside it, or on its border, changed (the `occ` dirty
  bit, §5.6).
- **The vertex shader reads the face's cell** (16 B, shared by its vertices):
  - the id selects the material row;
  - the temperature drives glow;
  - the fill gives the liquid-top height, with corners averaged over the 4 neighbouring columns (Minecraft's water
    corners);
  - the deco colour, if any.

  So a rising lake, cooling lava or a heated steel plate costs no remesh.
- **Cull.** It runs only when the camera moved or a chunk was remeshed. One compute pass tests each chunk-and-direction
  bucket against the frustum and against facing away, and compacts the visible faces into one list per pipeline
  (8 B entries: face plus chunk), with indirect arguments. Drawing uses `firstInstance = 0`, which needs no optional
  feature.
- **Counts on the real presets** (`crisp/faces.txt`): 41K exposed faces in the lab, 36K in the volcano, 136K in Dam
  Valley. The budget is 400K faces in view plus 64K flying cubes. That is a light load for a TBDR rasteriser.

### 3.3 The material function (one row per element, no shader branches)

| field | meaning | example (SAND) |
|---|---|---|
| `tones` | 2–4 sRGB tones; a cell picks one by a stable hash of its world coordinate (TPT's per-particle jitter, Noita's per-pixel variance) | `#dcbc74 #d3b16a #e6c886 #c4a35e` |
| `pattern` | one of 16–24 hand-made 16×16 RGBA8 pixel-art tiles in one `texture_2d_array` with mips: R luminance, G pseudo-bump, B sparkle mask, A emissive mask (or the alpha of a cutout) | `grain` |
| `surface` | `matte · gloss · metal · glass · liquid · emissive · foliage`; sets roughness, F0 (reflectance at normal incidence), wrap and the transmittance path | `matte` |
| `emit` | constant (lamps), or `blackbody` from T through the one incandescence table, with Kirchhoff's (1 − F0) | `blackbody` |
| `extinction`, `ior` | liquids and glass: RGB per metre (Beer–Lambert), with a minimum optical depth so a drop reads in its body colour | — |
| `weather` | sky-facing faces lighten (crag rock reads as terrain; fidelity) | 0 |

- **Per pixel:**
  - `tone = tones[hash(cell) & 3]`;
  - `albedo = tone · pattern.R`;
  - a small normal bend from `pattern.G`;
  - sparkle = `pattern.B` × the sun lobe, anchored to texels so it is stable without TAA;
  - emission = `emit(id, T) · pattern.A`, so lava's crust glows only in its cracks.
- **Deco** (TPT's dcolour) replaces the tone.
- **Metals** take F0 from the tone.
- **Patterns are crisp at 1 m and resolve to the tone at 40 m.** They are nearest-filtered when magnified and use
  trilinear mips when minified. That fixes the scale problem the render audit found by authoring at the scale the
  player actually sees.
- **Bevel.** An art-directed brightening and darkening within `w` of convex edges, using the face's edge mask, costs
  ~5 ALU. With `w = 0` it is Minecraft-flat; a small `w` reads as chunky objects. It is the main knob against
  "Minecraft clone" and is picked on the art sheet.
- **Foliage.** Leaf patterns are alpha cutouts drawn with alpha-to-coverage under MSAA. Canopies let light through and
  cast dappled shadows instead of being 3×3 green cubes (fidelity's leaf family, as a raster pattern).

### 3.4 The light function (every pixel: faces, particles, gas, three.js figures)

```
L = albedo · ( sun · max(n·l, 0) · shadow · underwater(p)  +  (skyIrr(n) · S + B) · ao )  +  emit(id, T)
  + specular (gloss, metal, liquid): GGX sun lobe · shadow  +  skyRad(r) · S · Fresnel
then fog toward skyRad(view) by distance and height; inside a liquid, that liquid's extinction
S, B = sky level and RGB block light from the light grid at p + ½n, through Minecraft's curve f / (4 − 3f)
```

- **Sun shadow.**
  - A 1024² depth16 shadow map, with 2048² as a Mac option. It is rasterised from the face slabs and the flying cubes,
    so a falling slab casts a shadow.
  - It is read with one hardware 2×2 PCF compare.
  - The whole map is redrawn at most 30 Hz, and only when an opaque face in the sun's view changed or the sun moved.
- **Sunlight under water.** It is multiplied by `exp(−σ·d)`, where d is the depth below that column's liquid top, from
  a 128² liquid-top map (lean). That gives coloured light in a tank without a translucent shadow pass.
- **Glass casts no shadow.**
- **Sky and block light** (Minecraft's model, per cell):
  - **Storage.** A per-cell rgba8 3D texture (8 MB at 128³): `a` is the sky level, `rgb` the coloured block light.
  - **Propagation.** `L = max(E, maxₙ(Lₙ − wₙ))` over 26 neighbours, with distance costs in 1/16-level units: face
    16, edge 23, corner 28. That gives rounder pools of light than Minecraft's diamonds. The cost doubles in liquid,
    and opaque cells hold 0.
  - **Sky sources.** Cells above each column's highest opaque blocker.
  - **Block sources.** `emit(id, T)` (lava, embers, hot steel, lamps, torches, FIRE, plasma, burning cells) and the
    first-person held torch.
  - **Update.** A warm start over dirty boxes (a change, dilated by the 16-cell light radius), at most 4 Jacobi
    iterations per tick. Stale light decays by at least one level per iteration, so any start reaches the
    full-recompute answer within 16 iterations. That makes the incremental update testable bit for bit.
  - **Lookup.** One trilinear tap per pixel. The grid has one value per cell, so nothing leaks through 1-cell walls.
- **AO** comes from the mesher's corner values: free at shading time and free of noise.
- **Specular.**
  - One GGX lobe for the sun.
  - Environment reflection is the sky's radiance in the reflected direction times the sky level, so a cave pool
    reflects the dark.
  - Metals tint it by F0.
- **One sky.** An art-directed analytic gradient with a sun disc and a scrolling cloud layer. It draws the backdrop
  and is used for ambient light, reflections and fog. Box mode stands on a ground plane under it. This fixes render B1
  (the navy void behind a blue-lit scene).
- **One emission function.** `emit(id, T)` is used for faces, particles, gas, light sources and reflections alike.
  This fixes render B2 (glow 3–6× brighter in reflections).

### 3.5 The translucent pass (1×, after the MSAA resolve)

- **Liquid and glass faces:**
  - a manual depth test against the resolved linear depth;
  - refraction: an offset lookup into the resolved colour, rejected where it lands on nearer geometry;
  - RGB Beer–Lambert absorption, found by walking the state from the surface to the resolved depth;
  - Fresnel against the one sky, plus the sun glint;
  - foam whitening from the cell's foam byte;
  - a scrolled 16×16 ripple normal map, scaled by agitation (|flux|).

  The walk takes ≤ 16 steps. A brick flagged "all one liquid" is crossed in one step, and the top cell is scaled by
  its fill.

  The walk ends at whatever is behind the water, three.js bodies included, so **a swimmer is tinted by their own
  depth** and a red mesh under 3 m of water goes dark teal (fixes render B3).
- **Opaque liquids** (lava, mercury, mud) are drawn as opaque faces in the MSAA pass. Lava uses the emissive crust
  pattern.
- **Gas:**
  - A per-cell gas texture (rgba8: colour × density and density; plus an r8 heat channel for emitters) is written only
    for gas bricks that changed.
  - It is marched at ½ resolution (¼ on the iPhone), ≤ 32 steps from the eye to the resolved depth, skipping bricks
    with no gas via the masks.
  - It is lit by the light grid and one shadow-map compare per sample. FIRE and plasma emit blackbody(T).
  - It is composited with a depth-aware upsample.

  Smoke filling a room stops at its walls; camera-facing puffs would show hard lines where they cut through walls.
- **Flames.** Flipbook HDR sprites (Halo 3, TF2):
  - spawned on burning exposed faces and on FIRE cells, at most 1 per face;
  - a pool of 8K on the Mac and 4K on the iPhone (render-only, so a device-dependent cap is fine);
  - softened against the resolved depth and added into bloom.
- **Sparks** are small additive sprites.

Why one 1× pass:
- Ending the MSAA pass to copy colour would store its samples: ~71 MB per Mac frame (judge 1 §2.2).
- Reading the resolved targets from a second pass costs nothing extra.
- That second pass is the one place where everything that needs scene depth (water, glass, smoke, flames) gets it.

### 3.6 Flying matter

- **Powder and debris particles** are instanced cell-sized cubes in the opaque MSAA pass, shaded by the same material
  function: a thrown clump looks like sand. Positions are interpolated between ticks, so motion is smooth at 120 Hz on
  ProMotion. The cubes are drawn into the shadow map.
- **Liquid particles** are velocity-stretched round droplets:
  - opaque, depth-writing impostors in the MSAA pass;
  - in body colour with a minimum optical depth (Noita's readability), plus a Fresnel rim;
  - sort-free and antialiased; one in front of a pool occludes it correctly.

  Screen-space fluid (depth and thickness splats, a narrow-range filter) is a P6 upgrade, gated on the phone budget.

### 3.7 Views, overlays, first person, far field

- **Data views.** Fragment variants of the face pipeline, at most 6 pipelines:
  - heat: T through TPT's colormap;
  - pressure: an air-grid slice plus a tint;
  - flow: liquid flux;
  - X-ray: faces drawn additive with depth off, nearly free in raster;
  - **cutaway**: a clip plane with a capped section (new, lean).
- **The plain view goes.** v2 is the cheap view.
- **Overlays** (cursor, brush, box edges, legends) are drawn in the canvas pass after tone mapping. Moving the mouse
  costs one small pass, where today it costs 109 frames of full marching (perf P6).
- **First person.**
  - The same pipeline. At 50+ px per cell, pattern texels are 2 cm, Minecraft's density.
  - Inside a liquid, fog uses that liquid's extinction over the part of each view ray below the column's surface.
  - three.js figures and viewmodels read the light grid and shadow map through TSL, as today's figures read GI. Their
    opaque colour and depth are composited into the MSAA pass by one depth-writing full-screen triangle (the port's
    Sync S1 order).
- **World far field.**
  - The same mesher runs on the generator's far summaries: LOD1 is one 1.2 m block per brick, LOD2 one 4.8 m block per
    4³ bricks. It runs per far chunk when the chunk is generated or summarised.
  - Shading is the same function, with a heightmap sun shadow.
  - Retired: `far.js`, the CPU surface-nets detail worker, `farTop` and `farShadow`. Near and far become one renderer.
- **Presentation effects** (main's `gfx/presentation*.js`, added after this design was written: Willow Cove's
  Animal Crossing gamemode bends the world into a rolling log).
  - In raster this is a few lines of the face vertex shader, as in Animal Crossing itself: every vertex drops by
    its view distance squared over the log's radius. Faces, figures, picking and the shadow map share one bend
    function; physics stays flat.
  - It replaces today's chord march through the volume and the far field, which costs a 17th texture unit and turns
    off the mesh cut while curved (main 61a82f0).

### 3.8 Resolution, anti-aliasing, the governor, crunch mode

- **4× MSAA in tile memory.**
  - The samples use `storeOp: discard`, and transient attachments where the browser offers them.
  - Resolve happens in tile memory to colour plus linear depth: 12 B per sample, under the 32 B per-sample limit.
  - No TAA, no jitter, no history buffers.
- **Base resolution:**
  - iPhone: 1.5× CSS pixels, 660×1434 (≈ 0.95M px).
  - Mac: 1.0× CSS pixels, 1512×982 (≈ 1.5M px), by default. Up to 1.5× if P1's power measurements allow it, since
    shading costs ~0.1 ms of ALU per million pixels here.
- **The governor** moves the render scale between 0.5× and 1.0× of the base to hold the frame budget:
  - it reads timestamp queries where they exist, and rAF lateness otherwise;
  - it uses hysteresis;
  - it never changes materials or light.
- **Crunch mode** (Cruelty Squad): ⅓ resolution, nearest-neighbour upscale, no MSAA. It is a style toggle and the
  cheapest phone setting.
- **120 Hz.** Chrome on ProMotion presents at 120 Hz. Safari caps rAF at 60 Hz by default, and Low Power Mode at 30.
  The tick stays at 60 Hz in every case.

## 4. Water and liquids

The model is fill plus face flux with weak compressibility: virtual pipes in 3D (Mei et al. 2007), combined with a
compressible cellular automaton (W-Shadow 2009). LEAN's CPU prototype validated it, and Noita's split between
particles and grid handles everything airborne.

### 4.1 Bulk liquid

- **A liquid cell holds:**
  - one liquid id;
  - `fill` as a u24 (1.0 = 2¹⁶; above 1.0 means compressed);
  - the flux on its +x, +y and +z faces (f16 each, owned by the cell);
  - a foam byte.
- **Per substep:** 3 per tick (180 Hz), each two gather-only dispatches over awake liquid bricks.
  1. **Flux.**
     - Each face's flux is accelerated by the head difference across it: `q += g·dt²·(hᵢ − hⱼ ± weight)`, then damped
       by viscosity.
     - `h = fill` for a cell with a free surface; `h = 1 + (fill − 1)/COMP` once compressed.
     - Compression is what carries pressure through lids, pipes and U-bends, with no special case.
     - Each cell computes its outflow limit K redundantly (never send more than you hold).
  2. **Gather.** `fill += Σ in·K_src − Σ out·K_self` in integers, so mass is exact by construction.
- **R13 numerical transport calibration.** Native limiting retains the complete stored finite f16 flux
  (active magnitudes are zero or at least ½) when forming proportions, then truncates **after** scaling by
  the existing downward-rounded 16-bit K. Limiter accounting and canonical gather/jet packets share the same
  exact u32 accepted-amount helper. Complete weights select the first largest face deterministically; its
  existing five-bit remainder apportions limited sources exactly before packet admission. Full K still
  returns `floor(max(q, 0))`.
  This changes accepted amounts, not the dead band, snapping, damping, yield, compression or acceleration.
  It is a numerical calibration, **not** byte equivalence, a global ≤1 cm guarantee or a proven native
  U-tube fix: the CPU fractional-weight ablation is not an exact GPU trajectory twin. The original native
  U-tube, partial-pool, tank-still and yield gates remain required at unchanged thresholds and deadlines.
  Separately, canonical packet admission rejects amounts above one sixth of frozen receiver headroom
  (saturating headroom at zero for already-full/overfull receivers). Rejection retains all source mass,
  heat and fuel and marks it busy. This defensive invariant covers largest-face tails independently of
  gravity; it is not prototype equivalence or a final-fill clamp, and ordinary full-K flows below that
  admission bound are unchanged. No buffer, pass, snapshot/tag ABI or material row changes.
- **Stiffness.**
  - Stability needs `COMP ≥ 3·g·dt²`. At 2 g in the historical 4-substep estimate, COMP ≈ 0.0034, so the hidden
    mass is ≈ 5 % on average at 30 cells deep.
  - The GPU bench may find a stiffer stable setting: fidelity's `ε = g·dt²` gives ≈ 1.7 %.
  - Only the top cell's fill is drawn, so compression never shows as a level.
  - Buckets, pumps and POV tools take **mass**, never "a cell", so a deep lake can't duplicate water.
  - If deep seas show it anyway, the remedy is an open-column hydrostatic head from a per-column prefix sum, which needs
    no compression at all in open water (risk 4, §12).
- **Rest and yield are one knob.**
  - A face whose drive is under its dead band drives nothing, and its flux decays.
  - Integer fills then reach a true fixed point instead of a ±1-quantum limit cycle, and the brick sleeps.
  - The prototype showed the dead band acting as a Bingham yield stress. So mud, slime and lava hold their slopes
    through a per-liquid dead band (lava's grows as it cools, until it sets).
  - Viscosity (Pa·s, from today's rows) is the damping.
  - This fixes liquids B9 with one mechanism, not two.
- **Films.** No sideways outflow onto dry ground from a cell under `MIN_FILM`, so a spill stops as a puddle with an
  edge.
  - The prototype at ⅛ spreads a 6³ spill into a round puddle 2–3 cm deep that rests at 14.5 s.
  - The value is an art knob between ⅛ and ¼ (picked on the motion sheet), so puddles read at god-view distance.
- **Two liquids.** One liquid per cell.
  - Flux flows only between cells of the same id, or into air.
  - A denser cell over a lighter one swaps in the block pass, so oil floats on water.
  - A part-filled cell under another liquid or a powder swaps upward, so bubbles rise (B15).
- **Partial cells and the rest of the sim.** Decided once, centrally:
  - heat capacity, reactions and buoyancy scale with fill;
  - a cell under 1/16 full counts as air for reactions;
  - lava meeting water: the pair reaction (steam + basalt or obsidian) consumes fill from both cells.

### 4.2 Airborne liquid

- **Falling stays in the grid.** A liquid cell with air below drains downward by vertical flux. A poured column stays
  a column, and nothing "lands" in mid-air (fixes B6 and B7).
- **Grid to particle.**
  - Sideways outflow faster than 1 m/s into a cell over open air leaves the grid as a particle. It carries exactly the
    transferred fill, at the Bernoulli speed of the static head behind the hole.
  - So a jet leaves at √(2gh) and follows the parabola. Torricelli is an outcome, not a rule: today's `ORIFICE_CV`
    head scan goes.
  - Blasts, kicks and tools throw liquid as particles.
- **Particle to grid.** Fill is deposited into the cell above the hit by an integer `atomicAdd` into a deposit
  accumulator, which the next flux pass gathers (order-free and deterministic).
- **Splashes.** An impact above 1 m/s (Ervine and Chanson's entrainment onset) throws a crown of droplets around the
  impact normal. Their total kinetic energy is ≤ 0.3 of the incoming; the rest becomes foam and heat (fixes B5,
  where today a splash multiplies energy by 160–210).

### 4.3 Foam

- A byte per liquid cell.
- It rises with particle impacts (by kinetic energy) and with flux shear and divergence (hydraulic jumps, wakes behind
  bodies).
- It is advected upwind by the gather and decays at Monahan's τ (2.54 s fresh, 3.85 s salt).
- A `foam` column on every liquid row means all 19 liquids can foam, saltwater included (fixes B12).
- Removed: the aeration pass, `moveSrc`, the out-of-place move and the Whitewater switch.

### 4.4 Evidence and acceptance

| test | today (GPU, 32³, measured) | prototype (60 Hz, 2 g, 3 substeps) |
|---|---|---|
| U-tube, left arm filled to 25 | left 25, right 5, *certified idle* | left 12.35, right 12.28 (Δ 2 cm); idle at 6.4 s; mass exact |
| partial top layer (3 layers + 150 cells) | 150 beads churn forever; 38 % of the grid awake | flat to 0.02 cell; idle at 4.3 s |
| puddle (6³ dropped on a floor) | 1-cell speckle, never rests | round puddle, 2–3 cm deep; idle at 14.5 s |
| tank → pipe, 27 deep | flow stops by step 300 with 27 cells of head | drains 972 → 150 cells in 60 s, still draining |
| 1-cell hole in a tank | single cells scattered over 20 cells of floor | one parabolic arc; exit speed 0.80·√(2gh) |

GPU acceptance (week 0 writes these as tests that fail on today's build):
- a U-tube equalises within one quantum;
- a tank drains through a 30-cell pipe to the pipe's level;
- a hole pours one unbroken stream within 5 % of the √(2gh) parabola at every head;
- a puddle rests, then AWAKE = 0;
- a pool of any volume rests flat;
- slope rises with yield;
- splash KE ≤ 0.3 × incoming;
- liquid plus particle mass is exact;
- a settled world with water in it certifies idle.

## 5. Physics

### 5.1 One clock, one g, real units

- **A fixed 60 Hz tick** with an accumulator, decoupled from presentation (Minecraft's game tick).
  - The grid, particles, heat, fuses, flame lives, tools and the first-person player all run on it.
  - Tool strengths are per second.
  - If the GPU falls behind, the world slows rather than the frame: at most 2 catch-up ticks per frame, then time
    dilates (Minecraft's lag).
  - This fixes physics B13: today the world's speed is the frame rate, and there are three clocks.
- **g = 19.6 m/s² (2 g)** for the grid, particles and the player: TF2's `sv_gravity 800` at 1 unit = 1 inch is
  ≈ 20 m/s².
  - It is one constant. Liquid compression scales with g·dt², which §4.1 already accounts for.
  - Each class has a terminal speed in m/s: dust and ash drift, grains and debris fall fast. A bulk run in the column
    pass falls at its class's run speed, not at a single grain's.
- **Speed setting** (TPT's): 1×, 2× or 4× ticks per second, with the cost shown beside it.
  - **Proposal: box mode at 2×, first person at 1×.** The miniature-photography rule: a miniature looks right when time
    runs √(scale) faster.
  - From the god view, the 38 m box reads as a ~10 m diorama, and √3.8 ≈ 2.

  | fall, before terminal speed | today (≈ 44 g, capped; est.) | 2 g, 1× | 2 g, 2× |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3 m (10 cells) | ~0.2 s | 0.55 s | 0.28 s |
  | 10 m | ~0.3–0.4 s | 1.0 s | 0.5 s |
  | the whole 38 m box | ~0.6–1 s | 2.0 s | 1.0 s |

  The motion sheet in P2 decides; changing it is one constant and one default.
- **Heat** runs on one declared accelerated clock (`HEAT_SPEED` ≈ 10⁴ × real diffusivity, as TPT's), because lava
  that took days to cool would be no fun.

### 5.2 State: 16 B per cell, one copy, updated in place

```
word0  id u8 · T u16 (¼ K steps, 0–16,383 K) · age u8 (fall age for runs; gas age; burn stage)
word1  aux u32 — liquids: fill u24 + foam u8
                 others:  life / fuel / ctype / latent-heat bank / spark state (meaning by kind)
word2  liquids: flux x, y (2 × f16)          others: free (wire state, charge)
word3  liquids: flux z (f16) + 16 free bits  others: free
```

- Brick-major order and the four index functions are kept (`docs/webgpu.md` C2).
- Every kernel goes through `loadCell` and `storeCell`, as `fetchA` and `writeState` are used today. So the packing can
  change without touching the ~40 first-person, world and tool kernels.
- **Alongside the cells:**

  | buffer | size |
  |---|---|
  | decoration plane (TPT's dcolour) | 4 B per cell, allocated lazily |
  | per-brick masks: any, solid, liquid, gas (64 bits each), double-buffered | 2 MB |
  | air grid (32 B per brick, 2 copies) | 2 MB |
  | particle records: 32 B each, a fixed cap of 64K everywhere, so peers agree | 2 MB |
  | claim table and deposit accumulator | small |
  | per-brick activity words and lists | kept from the port |

- **Against today:** 80 B per cell of state plus 76 B per cell of render fields. The giant volcano (6.3M cells) needs
  ~101 MB and fits the spec-default 128 MiB storage binding (closes the port's K2.3).

### 5.3 Three motion paths

| path | moves | how | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **column fall** | vertical runs of powder and debris with air or gas below | one thread per (x, z) column over its awake bricks, bottom up; a run moves `⌊s(age)⌋ − ⌊s(age−1)⌋` cells a tick, where s is the fall from rest at g capped at the run speed; a run landing on a slower run takes its age; landings emit impacts | a slab stays a slab, exactly on the ideal fall curve (prototype); painting sand in mid-air costs no particles; race-free because one thread owns a column |
| **block pass** | contact: topple, sink and float, gas buoyancy and diffusion, heat, phase changes, reactions, burning, sparks, breaking | the port's fused in-place 2×2×2 kernel, with its rules rewritten; the partition offset walks a fixed cycle of all 8 offsets; lookahead reads last tick's brick masks | race-free in place with one copy; every lower neighbour is reachable within a cycle (ends the quadrant lock and square heaps) |
| **particles** | anything with sideways speed: throws, debris, jets, splashes, wind-lifted sand, kicks, the physgun, first-person rounds that hit cells | ballistic under g, quadratic drag toward the air's velocity, a terminal speed per class, a swept DDA against the masks with no speed cap; land by claim (`atomicMin` of a deterministic key); losers retry next tick; above the cap, a source falls back to the grid deterministically | throws land exactly where their velocity says; ejecta forms a blanket; one law for a kicked clump, debris and a round (merging today's `pov/ballistics.js` 1 g world and the rays layer) |

Liquids move by flux (§4), and gases by the block pass plus drift with the air (P4).

### 5.4 The tick

```
1 particles   integrate · sweep · claim · deposit · splash          (≈ 2 dispatches)
2 column      falling runs, bottom up                                (1)
3 block       contact rules, in place, offset k = tick mod 8         (1)
4 liquid ×4   flux, then gather; deposits gathered; jets emitted     (8)
5 air         TPT air on 4³ bricks (P4); sleeps per air cell         (1–2)
6 activity    OR of every pass's possible|changed per brick → AWAKE/DRAWN lists, run counter (D5 kept),
              masks for the next tick, and the dirty bits upkeep reads: occ (remesh, light, shadow),
              emit (light only), gas (gas texture), fill (liquid-top map)   (≈ 5)
```

That is ≈ 19 dispatches, or ~30–35 counting Chrome's indirect-validation dispatches. A sleeping world costs 0.

### 5.5 The rules (the complete list)

- **R1 Fall.** Powders and debris with air or gas below fall as runs (column pass). A landing emits `KE = ½mv²`
  (m = ρ · 0.027 m³) for R8. Landings create no heat: `KE_TO_HEAT` (25× the literal value) goes.
- **R2 Topple.**
  - A supported grain in a block's top layer moves to an empty lower cell if its class's drop test holds and the cells
    below the target, read from the masks, allow it.
  - The classes are drop 1 (45°, free-flowing), drop 2 (63°, cohesive) and drop 3 (72°, clay, snow). Each row cites its
    measured angle, quantised to the nearest class.
  - Diagonal reach is hashed to a Moore share of 50 %, so heaps come out round. Prototype: a diagonal-to-axis radius
    ratio of 1.02, sand at 0.89 and flour at 1.79 rise per run.
  - The world generator takes its slopes from the same table, so generated terrain is already at rest when it loads.
- **R3 Sink and float.**
  - A denser movable over a less dense fluid swaps vertically at a rate set by density and viscosity.
  - Grains displace grains when the density ratio is over 2 (TPT's Weight): uranium sinks through sawdust.
  - Part-filled liquid under a powder swaps upward.
- **R4 Gas.**
  - Gases rise or sink against their neighbours by density, with ideal-gas thinning by T.
  - They diffuse by random swaps only while `age < diffusionAge`. Age resets on any disturbance: a neighbour change,
    or air velocity in P4.
  - So a still CO₂ pool settles and sleeps, and smoke, FIRE and plasma decay by `life` (fixes B11).
  - Air cells are a gas too: hot air rises by buoyant swaps, which is convection at cell scale.
- **R5 Heat.**
  - Each block's 12 internal faces exchange `⌊k·ΔT·dt⌋` in integer quanta, with harmonic-mean conductivity and
    `HEAT_SPEED`. Every face is internal on half the offsets, and the constant accounts for that.
  - The exchange is exactly conserving to the ¼ K rounding.
  - A sub-quantum ΔT moves nothing, so hot matter cools and then sleeps exactly.
  - The box's open sides and top exchange with ambient through a boundary conductance. There is no global pull to
    ambient and no per-scene `airT`.
- **R6 Phase change.** Rows give `hot`, `cold` and `crush` (in P4, crush reads the air grid), with the latent-heat bank
  in `aux` (kept). Lava remembers what it melted from in `ctype` (kept). Steam condenses onto cold faces.
- **R7 Reactions.**
  - Noita-format pair rows, evaluated against all 3 in-block partners per tick (today 1, which caps every reaction at
    1/6).
  - `chance` is a rate per second, corrected for pairing and the offset cycle.
  - Product temperature comes from the energy balance, capped at the products' flame temperature. That fixes the
    5,300 °C hydrogen–oxygen flame.
- **R8 Fire.**
  - **Ignition.** Retain stochastic contact ignition (`fire/60` per call) and certainty at the fuel's ignition
    temperature. Ordinary fuel also activates deterministically beside live, hot FIRE or burning fuel whose
    decoded post-pair temperature reaches the recipient's existing ignition threshold. FIRE must have life
    remaining and be at least 350°C; burning fuel must have life remaining. Invalid, done, pending-conversion
    and DETONATE/NEUTRON-requested sources do not qualify for this additional route. Low-flash fuel still
    requires its own flash temperature for deterministic contact activation. All routes retain the recipient's
    six-face oxygen/CO₂ gates and existing finite-fuel heat accounting; no ignition temperature jump is added.
    The pre-self snapshot prevents intra-call cascades but retains one-tick source-extinction latency.
    TPT's `FIRE.cpp` motivates contact versus bulk ignition; its `Flammable/1000` contact rule is stochastic.
  - **Burning** is a state of the fuel, its burn stage kept in `age`:
    - it spends `fuel` J from `aux`;
    - it heats itself and its neighbours, capped at `flameT`;
    - it releases SMOKE and CO₂ cells, plus FIRE plume gas into the open air above at a capped rate;
    - it ends as `burn.into`;
    - it is smothered by CO₂, or by having no exposed face against oxygenated air.
  - **FIRE** stays a paintable element: a short-lived (~0.5–1 s), hot, buoyant gas that ignites what it touches and
    decays to smoke.
  - Flames are drawn as sprites (§3.5).
  - **Acceptance gate:** two 1,000 °C flames must light a wooden block within a second or two (B9);
    the unchanged six-bay `flameWood` criterion remains at least 5/6 lit. CPU hot-contact witnesses do not
    establish moving-fixture acceptance; parent GPU checks also own `woodBurns` and the Campfire pre-heat gate.
- **R9 Break.**
  - A solid breaks into `breakInto` when the impact kinetic energy reaching it is at least its `toughness` (J), or
    (P4) when the air-pressure difference across a face times the face area exceeds its `strength` (Pa).
  - Liquids never break solids by impact.
  - Gold breaks steel only when a 521 kg cell actually carries the energy (fixes B7 and B8).
- **R10 Blasts.**
  - An explosive releases `E · BLAST_SCALE` J (10⁻³, declared: a 27-litre cell of real TNT would level the site).
  - **P2: immediate damage**, Teardown-style:
    - cells inside the blast radius break when the energy reaching them exceeds their toughness;
    - the energy is E/4πr², shielded by solid cells along the line of sight (a DDA through the masks);
    - loose cells launch as particles along r with the impulse;
    - hot products remain.

    So small charges make crisp craters with an ejecta rim.
  - **P4:** the energy also enters the air grid as a round shock (R12).
- **R11 Sparks.** TPT's electricity automaton (`electricity.js`), kept as is, over block pairs.
- **R12 Air (P4).** TPT's `Air.cpp` in 3D on 4³ bricks, conservative:
  - `v −= ∇p·dt/ρ`, `p −= κ·∇·v·dt`, and semi-Lagrangian advection of velocity and heat;
  - an open-face fraction per brick face, computed from its cells;
  - a loss of 0.9999 per tick (TPT's `AIR_PLOSS`);
  - the speed of sound slowed to ~50 m/s, so the explicit step is stable at 60 Hz and a shock visibly crosses the box
    in ~0.7 s;
  - blasts add energy, and burning and boiling add gas;
  - wind, vacuum and cyclone tools set the air, and matter follows it: particles drag toward its velocity, a loose
    surface cell under air faster than its `lift` detaches (saltation), and gas cells drift with it.
- **R13 Liquid flux.** §4.

### 5.6 Rest and sleep

- **Reporting.** Every pass ORs `possible | changed` into its brick's word: one workgroup reduction, then one
  `atomicOr`.
- **Sleeping.** A brick goes quiet after one full aligned 8-tick offset cycle in which nothing was possible in it or
  its 26 neighbours. The port's AWAKE and DRAWN lists, run counter and invariant (D5) are reused unchanged. The extra
  8 ticks (133 ms) of awake time cost nothing.
- **Why it can be reached:** integer temperature, integer fill and the dead bands. The prototype certifies idle in
  every case except the still-draining pipe:

  | case | idle after |
  |---|---|
  | sand heap | 1.55 s after the pour ends |
  | flour heap | 2.65 s after the pour ends |
  | U-tube | 6.4 s |
  | partial layer | 4.3 s |
  | puddle | 14.5 s |

- **What goes.** The hand-written mirrors (`inertNear`, `inertSelf`), the six `REST_*` tolerances and `quietOk`. So
  does the bug class behind physics B1 (heaps that never sleep) and E8.
- **Air cells** sleep on their own under quanta of p and v. A blast wakes 1/64 of the cells, where today it wakes every
  brick for 260 steps.
- **Visible change** is reported separately as the dirty bits in §5.4. Upkeep reads only those, never the step
  counter (fixes perf P1–P3).

### 5.7 Determinism and multiplayer

- **Kept** (W21):
  - hashed random streams seeded by world cell, tick and salt;
  - `u32ToF32`;
  - integer atomics only;
  - the list-permutation test (I2.1).
- **New:**
  - claims use `atomicMin` of a key derived from (particle id, tick);
  - deposits use integer `atomicAdd`;
  - particle ids come from (spawn cell, tick, rule), never from buffer slots;
  - the particle cap is a fixed 64K, so every peer runs the same game.
- **Multiplayer:**
  - brick diffs from the writers' marks;
  - particles in flight are streamed as seeded emission events that clients integrate;
  - the host is authoritative for claims;
  - the codec moves to v2, with a converter for old saves.

## 11. The build plan

Every phase:
- ships to tpt3d.codyh.xyz;
- is visibly better;
- is judged **side by side against the current game**: same scene, camera, seed and time, as stills and 5 s clips,
  on the user's Mac and iPhone, plus the numbers named for that phase.

**Before acceptance**, a phase runs behind `?engine=v2`. Until P5, a frozen build of today's game stays deployed at
`/v1/` for live comparison.

### Week 0: the ruler

No player-visible change; at most a week.

- **`tools/side-by-side.mjs`.** Two builds (`/v1/` and the candidate), fixed cameras. It captures stills and 5 s clips
  in the container (SwiftShader, for reference) and on the Mac and iPhone (for judging).
- **The shot list.**
  - Stills: the lab, the lab tank, the volcano god view, the volcano flank, Dam Valley, the island, a Descent
    corridor.
  - **Action shots** (judge 0): a burning forest, a smoke-filled room, a buried charge mid-blast, a dam break, a pour
    from a hole, a sand avalanche, lava meeting water, a first-person swim.
- **The outcome bench.** LEAN's prototype tests ported to the GPU harness as tests that fail on today's build:
  - U-tube, puddle, partial layer, pipe, hole jet;
  - heap angle and sleep, slab, throw;
  - flame lights wood;
  - blast symmetry, sealed vessel;
  - settled world certifies idle;
  - mass census.

  `lean-sim.mjs` stays as the CPU tuning bench.
- **The raster bench page.** The lab's real faces, drawn through the MSAA opaque pass plus the 1× translucent pass at
  native resolution, on the iPhone and the Mac. It records:
  - pass times;
  - each pipeline's cold compile time;
  - transient attachments present or not;
  - 1 versus 2 frames in flight on Safari 26.x.

  **Go/no-go:** if MSAA costs more than 1.5× a TAAU-at-⅔ variant of the same scene, the default anti-aliasing falls
  back to TAAU (judge 1, gate 1).
- **Baselines.** `power.mjs` runs of today's build on the Mac, so every "today" number becomes a measurement.

### P1: New look (the new renderer on today's physics)

The highest-impact, lowest-risk change. Size L, shipped in two drops.

- **Builds:**
  - the mesher, reading today's A buffer (id, T) through a v1 decoder of `loadCell`;
  - faces, cull and the opaque MSAA pass;
  - the material and light functions, the shadow map, the per-cell light grid, one sky with a ground;
  - the translucent pass:
    - liquids as whole cells with flat, corner-averaged tops;
    - isolated airborne liquid cells drawn as droplets, not cubes;
  - the gas march from today's gas cells;
  - flame sprites on FIRE and burning cells;
  - the data views and cutaway, overlays after tone mapping, the governor, crunch mode;
  - **the art pass and its sheet.**

  The first drop covers the box and the world's near window; the second adds the world's far field as LOD faces.
  World is the default desktop session, so it gets the new look before v2 goes default there.
- **Visibly better because:** everything you look at changes, and the physics stays the same. The view, fields, GI,
  the shadow trace and the TAA tail leave the frame: ≈ 9–17 ms off a busy Mac frame (est.). A busy lab goes from
  18–28 ms to ~9–13 ms; the rest is today's sim.
- **Becomes the default** when the user prefers it on the sheet. Pours still move like today's (in beads) until P2.
  If that bothers the user more than today's look, the flag stays until P2. Judge 0's rule holds: never default-on
  today's beaded liquids redrawn as cubes against the user's wishes.
- **Exit:**
  - the user prefers the pairs;
  - view plus upkeep ≤ 1.5 ms on the Mac and ≤ 3 ms on the iPhone (measured in settled scenes);
  - a still camera draws 0 frames;
  - a pointer move draws one canvas pass.
- **Deletes, once default:**
  - `render.js`'s volume and every `shaders/gfx/*` material;
  - `fields.js`, GI, the shadow DDA;
  - TAA/TAAU/CAS, the detail tiers, `detailGate`, `coldShader`;
  - the plain view, `MOBILE_DEFAULTS`, the stacked tone curves;
  - the far marcher.

### P3: Water, all of it

Size M.

- **Builds:**

  - foam from impacts and shear;
  - ripples from agitation;
  - the underwater view and hooks for its sound;
  - first-person buoyancy from mass;
  - the deep-water stiffness check (hidden mass; the hydrostatic-head remedy if it shows);

- **Exit:**
  - the hole jet lands within 5 % of the √(2gh) parabola at every head;
  - a dam break surges, then rests;
  - splash KE ≤ 0.3 × incoming;
  - all 19 liquids foam according to their rows;
  - mass is exact;
  - the user prefers the dam break, pour, sea and lava-meets-water pairs.

### P4: Air, fire and blasts

Size M–L.

- **Builds:**
  - the TPT air grid on 4³ bricks (conservative, sound at ~50 m/s);

  - breaking by Δp;
  - vacuum, wind and cyclone acting on the air;
  - gas and smoke drift, saltation, pressure from boiling, convection;
  - the pressure and wind views.
- **Exit:**
  - a blast's diagonal/axis ratio within 15 %;
  - a sealed vessel keeps ≥ 90 % after 10 s;
  - vacuum pulls;
  - the user prefers the burning forest, smoke-filled room and charge mid-blast clips;
  - a blast wakes the air grid, not the whole box.

### P5: Worlds and the phone

Size L.

- **Exit:**
