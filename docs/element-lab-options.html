Design proposal · not an implemented map

The every-element lab

An indoor, walkable collection that builds itself from the element registry. Physical state chooses the wing; reusable hazard tags choose how each exhibit is stored.

Decision needed: choose the building layout and powder display. These drawings are schematic, not engine screenshots or tested containment designs.

Recommendation: A, the museum spine. Square viewing windows for the collection, catwalks over liquid vats, and a small sand hopper beside the powder samples. Hazardous specimens get visibly different modules without being moved out of their physical-state wing.

Three building directions

Recommended

A · Museum spine

Feel: a readable research museum. Long, roofed galleries with a sign outside every square window.

Play: break a powder shelf, watch grains spill; step off a railed catwalk into a liquid vat.

Tradeoff: less theatrical than a giant central chamber, but easiest to extend without rebuilding the entrance.

Most theatrical

B · Reactor atrium

Feel: a huge indoor machine room. Tall powder silos, stacked gas windows, vats below an upper walkway.

Play: overlook many exhibits at once, then descend into individual galleries.

Tradeoff: visual density, roof/camera visibility and vertical navigation need more work. An expanding catalog needs additional atrium modules.

Most experimental

C · Containment blocks

Feel: a slightly alarming test facility. Each bay has an observation window, service hatch and its own hazard treatment.

Play: isolate a spill or deliberately breach one chamber. A large sign stands in for an unavailable specimen.

Tradeoff: more walking and walls; fewer panoramic views. Doors and walls alone must not be advertised as neutron shielding.

Make powders worth watching

1 · Square specimen window

A shallow, glass-fronted cell, filled with grains. A retaining box keeps a square silhouette; an unsupported powder cube would slump.

Best for comparing all materials. Break the window to release the sample.

2 · Hopper + catch tray

A small upper hopper drains through a neck into a visible heap. Different grains make different piles.

Recommended companion to the static sample. A finite pour can settle; a perpetual one needs a real source and drain, not imaginary recirculation.

3 · Destructive test bay

A contained release or drop experiment shows how a material falls, scatters or reacts. Stable collection display remains separate.

Most interactive. Triggering and resetting are extra mechanics, so I would defer them until the collection works.

Liquids: use broad, open-topped vats with a side window and a catwalk over the rim. Ordinary liquids are accessible; a dangerous or unstable liquid may require a closed vat. Jumping in is possible where open, not necessarily survivable.

Gases: sealed square-window chambers with vertical room to rise or settle. Clear gases get honest labels, not an invented visible tint. Flow arrows or schematic graphics must be labeled as diagrams.

Solids: blocks on plinths. Electronics sit on isolated demonstration benches; nearby batteries must not accidentally energize every sample.

One catalog, not a second hand-maintained map

Registry + reusable tags→Exhibit catalog→Category wings + storage modules→Geometry + plaques + directory

What stays DRY

  • Category: derive from kind, not the palette's hand-written lists. Gunpowder remains in Powders, with an explosive tag.
  • Facts: read spawn temperature, phase changes, ignition, descriptions and other existing fields. Nuclear data remains authoritative in its existing module.
  • New metadata: reusable hazards and storage needs belong to element definitions, not an element-name switch in the map. Explicitly review exceptional behavior that existing fields cannot describe.
  • Placement: deterministic slots within category wings; append new material IDs in order. Compute a finite, aligned footprint at map creation, with an expansion path rather than a fixed specimen count. This is automatic catalog growth, not an infinite allocation.
  • Unknown: an unsupported tag or unreviewed specimen gets a visible “not displayed” plaque and validation failure. It never silently inherits “safe in glass.”

Separate three questions

What is it? Powder, liquid, gas, solid, or a non-matter effect.

What can it do? Example vocabulary: flammable, oxidizer, corrosive, shock-sensitive, fissile, neutron-emitting, annihilating, matter-consuming, self-decaying, replicating.

What does this exhibit need? Example requirements: a supported heap, sealed volume, temperature band, isolated wiring, limited sample quantity or no persistent specimen.

Tags can combine. A cold flammable liquid must satisfy both requirements. Storage conflicts or missing support produce a reasoned plaque-only exhibit.

Adding an element should require only its normal definition and metadata review. New containment behavior may need a new shared module, not a custom map edit per element.

Saved worlds need a policy: fresh generation picks up the current catalog. Updating an already edited lab must preserve player changes; catalog versioning and expansion cannot be assumed to happen safely without migration tests.

Containment must match the game's actual rules

ExampleProposed exhibitHonesty constraint
Sand / stone / ashWindowed specimen bin and optional finite hopper.Powders settle; they do not stay as floating cubes.
Liquid nitrogen / snow / dry iceInsulated cold-storage module, a temperature gauge, and visible cooling machinery if cooling is implemented.Insulation slows warming; it does not maintain a setpoint. A battery is not a refrigerator. Use a plaque until persistent storage is supported and tested.
Lava / steamHeated vat or chamber with an isolated service area.Do not freeze the simulation to keep an exhibit looking stable. Maintaining temperature needs an actual mechanism.
Acid / caustic gasAcid-resistant container; sealed chamber for gas.This game's glass is acid-proof, but still fragile. Chemical resistance is not blast resistance.
Gunpowder / nitroglycerinSeparated, quiet, non-igniting storage, appropriate to the actual trigger.“Explosive” is not one containment recipe. Nitroglycerin is sensitive to movement/impact.
Uranium / plutoniumDistinct labeled nuclear exhibits; start conservatively with tiny specimens or plaques.Criticality depends on quantity and surroundings. Water can make plutonium more reactive. A thicker wall does not automatically stop neutrons.
Antimatter / singularitySpecial compatible containment only if proven; otherwise an empty station with an explanation.Antimatter has specific compatible materials. A singularity consumes matter and changes with time even if its enclosure survives.
Fire / plasma / photons / neutronsExplained effect exhibits; optional explicitly labeled source-driven demonstrations.A continuously replaced effect is not a stable specimen. Photons/neutrons are not ordinary voxel materials.

Signs: quick read first, full facts nearby

Illustrative plaque · exact layout not decided

Liquid nitrogen LN2

LIQUIDCRYOGENICTEMPERATURE-SENSITIVE

Boils near −196 °C. Warms into ordinary air in this simulation.

Storage: needs sustained cooling. Insulation alone only delays boiling.

If cooling is unavailable: “No persistent sample — it warms and boils away.”

Shared facts, two levels

At a glance: name, abbreviation, state, hazard chips, specimen status and one useful behavior.

On inspection: description; spawn and live temperature where meaningful; modeled freezing/melting/boiling or other transitions and products; ignition/trigger; density relative to water; thermal/electrical behavior; containment explanation.

Do not invent missing facts. Show “not modeled” or “not applicable,” never a default 0 °C. A hot transition is not necessarily boiling: it can be decomposition.

Plaque-only stations still show the material's identity, all relevant tags and the specific reason no specimen is present.

Code evidence and important implementation limits
  • Baseline: fetched origin/main, commit 2beac9a082b50e2370f818af332f5681753dd359. No gameplay files changed for this proposal.
  • src/elements.js: CPU inventory found 95 matter entries: 41 solids, 23 powders, 19 liquids, 11 gases and air. Seven entries are absent from palette groups, including air and native terrain materials. Enumerating the palette would miss these and duplicate others.
  • src/elements.js and src/rays.js: photons and neutrons are separate particle tools; Spark is also an action rather than a persistent material. Include them as effect exhibits, but do not turn every editor tool or weapon into a specimen.
  • src/world/scenes/labWorld.js: existing deterministic room generator and glass tanks are useful precedent. It is intentionally unstable in places and does not supply this collection or a fully enclosed roof.
  • src/world/scenes/index.js: world scenes are deterministic CPU/GPU functions. An exhibit catalog must feed both generation and the far view rather than drift into parallel hand-written lists.
  • src/signs.js, src/shaders/probe.js: current signs have an 80-character limit and a 64-sign cap. Ninety-five full fact plaques cannot simply be stuffed into the existing sign tool. Generate a catalog of plaques but render/probe only nearby ones; preserve user-created signs.
  • src/ui/card.js: existing cards already derive some facts, but do not cover all phase rules. Extract shared fact formatting for cards and exhibit inspection.
  • src/rays.js: nuclear cross-sections and neutron emission are existing data. Reuse rather than maintain another list of radioactive materials.
  • src/v2/contact.js and src/shaders/v2/particles.js: active material/particle behavior must be checked here. Historical documentation and legacy shader references alone are not current runtime proof.
  • src/mapKind.js: a big map simulates a moving window, not the entire building at once. Label behavior honestly; do not imply every distant exhibit keeps running.
Prior art: what transfers, and what does not
  • The Powder Toy: glass implementation exposes category, physical properties, transitions and special update behavior in one definition. That property-driven approach transfers. Its glass passes neutrons and can shatter; it is not a universal safety window.
  • TPT: temperature sensor and heat switch provide established sensing and switched thermal-conduction patterns. These support visible control machinery as a design vocabulary. They do not establish a refrigerator in this project.
  • EnganK: Temp control methods, save 2634656, linked in the creator's heating/cooling discussion, is attributable temperature-control prior art. The save's internals were not downloaded or simulated; no circuit compatibility or performance claim follows.

The building layouts and storage-module composition above are proposals adapted to this codebase, not reproductions of a verified universal material museum.

Proposed first implementation slice and checks
  1. Agree on a layout and powder display. Keep the existing Lab and Lab Complex as separate maps.
  2. Review every material's reusable metadata and create a catalog entry for every material plus the named effect exhibits. No silent exclusions.
  3. Build one representative indoor section: ordinary powder, ordinary liquid, gas, cold material, hot material, explosive, nuclear material and a plaque-only exotic. This tests the shared recipes, not a hard-coded showcase list.
  4. Expand the same generator to the complete catalog. Generate signs, directory and rooms from that one catalog.
  5. CPU checks: exact-once coverage, hidden entries, stable addresses, category assignment, combined/unknown tags, unsupported containment, fact accuracy and growth beyond the initial capacity.
  6. Containment checks: track identity, sample quantity, temperature/pressure and escape into neighboring bays. State the tested duration; finite tests do not prove indefinite stability.
  7. Gameplay checks: enclosed roof, doors and catwalk clearance, accessible vats, readable nearby plaques and correct generation/far-view behavior. Follow the repository's 25-second total GPU-slot limit.
Choose: A / B / C for the building, and 1 / 2 / 3 for powders. Suggested answer: “A, with square specimen windows and small finite hoppers.”
Design only. Containment recipes, cooling machinery and performance have not been implemented or GPU-tested.
