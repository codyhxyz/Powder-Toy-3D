I did not deliver the Noita map you asked for.

You asked me to take Noita’s terrain and fold it into a recognizable 3D world. I made a thin, curved extrusion and split it into loading slices. That is a materially different result.

I then treated source-data checks and successful rendering as proof that the reconstruction was right. They were not. This page shows what the current deployment actually does.

Why you saw those four problems

“Why does Coal Pits have like seven levels?”

I split Coal Pits into four loading slices. From the initial arrival, the remaining first Holy Mountain slice, those four Coal slices, and the next two Holy Mountain slices make seven loaded pieces before Snowy Depths.

The code calls each piece a “Level” and shows an arrival banner again. These are engine chunks, not seven Noita levels. I exposed a streaming detail as level design. This explains the code’s counts; I did not record the exact sequence you played.

“Why did I keep finding chutes to drop down?”

The conversion repeats the same 2D cross-section across the whole depth. A 2D gap therefore becomes a full-depth slot; a 2D shaft becomes a chute-like channel. I also closed the front and back of the ribbon with artificial walls.

I have not traced your individual drops back to source coordinates. I cannot honestly claim that every chute you encountered was a particular original Noita shaft. The extrusion itself promotes that corridor-and-drop experience.

“There were no folds. It was all forwards.”

The “fold” is one constant, 270-degree bend spread over 9,216 source pixels, with a radius of about {{RADIUS}} cells. Over 128 source pixels, it turns only 3.75 degrees.

The descent itself never bends into the new axis. There are no section-by-section folds or authored 3D return routes. Your observation is consistent with the algorithm I shipped.

“It is completely unrecognizable as Noita.”

I used generic physical materials and voxel shading, omitted Noita’s background art, saved entities, movable props, and scripts, and placed conspicuous non-native walls around a thin ribbon.

The source is also one archived, previously played world—not a pristine reference seed. Real material input does not excuse a result that loses the game’s spatial and visual identity. This is not just a small polish problem.

Can I prove it is a straight port?

What I got wrong—and what the checks actually proved

The first version arranged selected room assets with invented connections. You rejected that correctly. The second, current version uses actual saved terrain, but applies the inadequate ribbon conversion shown above.

Neither result fulfilled your request for a recognizable, deliberately folded 3D Noita main descent.

What I verified

  • The decoded material pixels match the saved chunks.
  • The coordinate transform has a matching inverse.
  • Loading overlaps contain matching source data.
  • Two live scenes start and render without GPU errors.

What I did not establish

  • That a player recognizes Noita’s geography.
  • That the folds are meaningful or perceptible.
  • That loading slices feel like one continuous biome.
  • That the route preserves Noita’s exploration experience.

Salvageable: the source decoder, native material records, provenance checks, and editable pipeline stages.

Needs redesign: the thin-ribbon geometry, its artificial enclosure, the way loading chunks become “levels,” and the missing spatial/art direction.

This page explains the failure. It does not fix the map. No game geometry or gameplay code was changed for this report.
